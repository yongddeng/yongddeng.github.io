The readings are the bold layer’s: its sums’ second moment measured over 256 inputs, and the same worked out from what the layer reads, the fan-in times Var W times E a², plus σb². Under a norm, E ẑ² too, which the norm holds at 1.

The readings are the ring’s share in its top two components at the bold layer, the Jacobian’s mean σ² against mean field’s, and how many of its singular values lie within a factor of two of 1.

One input x goes through the stack, and three of its layers are laid out on the ground, the input nearest. Each white line holds a layer’s 64 units as dots: grey the input, navy a layer’s sums z = W a + b, green what the activation makes of them, a. The ground is coloured by how densely the values of 256 inputs lie, in the colours of the lines it runs between, and the dashed lines hold the middle 95% of them.

The thin grey lines are the river’s flow: each tenth of the values, rank to rank, from the input up. They run straight between a layer’s z and a, where the activation moves each unit along its own path, and dotted between an a line and the next z, where the weights mix the units and only the rank carries over. There the river only fades from one line’s colour to the next.

Of the weights’ links only those into the ringed unit are drawn, one a term W a of its sum, thicker the larger, solid where the term adds and dashed where it takes away. So its sum shows as a tug of war. The sheets are each layer’s W, a pixel an entry, darker the larger. Point at a dot to ring its unit, drag to turn, and Run to walk the layers.

With a norm on, each layer has a third line between z and a: ẑ, in blue, each input’s sums normalised over the layer’s 64 units, which is what the activation reads. The river runs straight from z to ẑ, since the norm moves an input’s units by one shift and one scale, and keeps their order.

Each layer’s second moment E z², the mean of its squared sums over 64 units and 256 inputs, as dots on a log axis. The line is mean field’s q. It starts at σw² + σb², and each layer takes it to σw² E a² + σb², with a the activation of a Gaussian z of variance q, or under a norm of a standard normal ẑ, the same at every layer. Where it has settled by the last layer, the dashed level is its fixed point q*. The larger dot is the bold layer.

The red dots are the backward pass’s E δz², δz = ∂ℓ/∂z, for a loss whose gradient at the last layer’s a is drawn standard normal, as mean field takes it (Schoenholz et al. 2017). Going down, each layer multiplies a unit’s gradient by its own φ′(z) and sums it down W’s columns, so mean field’s E δz² gains χ = σw² E φ′² a layer, the χ of Shape.

The activation at the bold layer. The dots on its curve are that layer’s 64 units for the one input, each where its sum put it, or under a norm its ẑ, and the larger one is the ringed unit, with its way through dashed. Under the curve is the ridge of what it reads, the layer’s z or ẑ, over 256 inputs. At its right is the ridge of the a it makes of them, which the next layer reads, beside a strip that marks the activation’s range. Dashed is the curve’s slope φ′, the gate the backward pass goes through: a unit far out on tanh’s flat ends, or below ReLU’s 0, passes almost nothing back.

A ring of 96 inputs, u cos θ + v sin θ with u and v random, orthogonal and of unit variance a unit, goes through a plain stack of N = 64 units a layer. Each layer’s ring is drawn on its own top two principal components, turned to match the layer before, so the tube shows the ring’s shape and size layer by layer. Poole et al. (2016) drew theirs on three components, at layers 5 and 10.

The ring’s length is the sum of its 96 steps. The distance between two nearby inputs grows by √χ a layer, with χ = σw² E φ′(√q ξ)² at that layer’s q, φ the activation and ξ a standard normal. Under a norm the activation reads ξ itself and the norm divides by √q, so χ = σw² E φ′(ξ)² / q. In chaos the steps grow until neighbours are as far apart as any two inputs, √(2q*(1 − c*)) a unit, and the drawn length levels off while the ring’s own length keeps growing.

The input-output Jacobian ∂zL/∂z1 is taken at one input scaled to start at q*. Mean field gives the mean of its σ², χ1 a layer, but not their spread. Orthogonal weights with tanh near q* = 0 keep them near 1, the dynamical isometry of Pennington, Schoenholz and Ganguli (2017). Gaussian weights spread them over decades. ReLU’s inactive units leave half or more at 0, since the Jacobian’s rank is at most the fewest units active in any layer.
